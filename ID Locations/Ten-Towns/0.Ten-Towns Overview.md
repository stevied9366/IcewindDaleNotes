##### **History**

Over 400 years old, founded by immigrants all over Faerun in search of escape or adventure. They built a trading post where [[Bryn Shander]] now stands.

One-by-one, settlements sprung up on the shores of [[Maer Dualdon]], [[Lac Dinneshere]], and [[Redwaters]]

Orcs and other monsters compelled the lakeside towns to turn [[Bryn Shander]] from a modest hilltop trading post into a walled town capable of defending all Ten-Towners if (and when) the worst comes


##### **Culture**

Most towns contain trace evidence of the immigrant cultures, carved into houses, statues, and other fixtures

Most residents remain indoors while not working due to the cold, making each settlement deathly quiet. Most people are bundled up in so much cold clothing as to be barely recognizable.

The population of Ten-Towns has dwindled due to Auril’s winter spell, and heightened rivalries that have existed for years, as competition for resources became increasingly intense. The alliance won’t hold if the mounting tribalism continues to threaten the common good.

To keep warm, there are not a lot of options:

- [[Good Mead]], [[Lonelywood]], and [[Termalaine]] burn wood salvaged from nearby forests
- Wood is too precious for the other towns, so whale oil is used in lamps and small stoves
	- Whale oil is bought from whalers who live on the shores of the Sea of Moving Ice. Whaling is a lucrative (and dangerous) business in Icewind Dale

  
Fishing boats are simple in Ten-Towns. The ships fly the flags of their towns and provide fish for the whole community

Smallest are rowboats and single-masted skiffs that require careful handling and avoid capsizing

Larger, twin-masted cogs and keelboats with single decks handle the wind and waves better


##### **Location**

Most of the ten towns, except [[Bryn Shander]], are built on shores of three big lakes

Largest population of knucklehead trout is in [[Maer Dualdon]], the deepest of lakes

[[Redwaters]], the shallowest lake, freezes almost completely in the winter, making fishing difficult

[[Lac Dinneshere]] catches the worst of the winds blowing off the [[Reghed Glacier]] to the east, and has the roughest waters

All these lakes have small thermal vents keeping them from freezing completely


##### **Travel**

Most Ten-Towners travel town to town on foot

Transporting more goods usually requires wooden sleds pulled by dogs or domesticated axe beaks as pack animals

* **Dogsleds** - 20 gp, 300 lbs, room for one drive
* **Sled Dog (wolf)** - 50 gp, can pull 360 lbs - Sled dogs require a short rest after pulling a sled for 1 hour, or they gain 1 level of exhaustion
* **Axe Beaks** - 50 gp, Large beast - Its splayed toes allow it to run across snow, and can carry a little over 400 lbs


##### **Gear**

Players can buy any item in the Equipment chapter in Ten-Towns

**Potions** are 50 gp each

Magic items are special case (below)

Additionally, PCs can buy specific winter gear:

- **Cold Weather Clothing** - 10gp, 5lbs - This outfit consists of a heavy fur coat or cloak over layers of wool clothing, as well as a fur-lined hat or hood, goggles, and fur-lined leather boots and gloves. As long as cold weather clothing remains dry, its wearer automatically succeeds on saving throws against the effects of [extreme cold](https://2014.5e.tools/trapshazards.html#extreme%20cold_dmg)
- **Crampons** - 2gp, 1/4 lb - A metal plate with spikes strapped to the sole of a boot. A creature wearing these cant fall prone while moving across slippery ice
- **Snowshoes** - 2gp, 4 lb - reduces likelihood of wearer getting stuck in the deep snow

  
##### **Magic**

Characters who want magic items or the services of a spellcaster are usually out of luck

May be able to find common magic items for sale

Common magic items cost 100 gp where available

A friendly spellcaster (one per hundred population) may cast spells on their behalf (1st - 3rd Level only)

Friendly spellcaster charges 25 gp for a 1st-level spell, 50 gp for a 2nd-level, and 150 gp for a 3rd-level, plus any expensive material components

  
##### **Council of Speakers**

Each town is an independent settlement that elects a leader, or speaker, to represent its interests at meetings of the [[Council of Speakers]], which are infrequent and take place at the Council Hall in [[Bryn Shander]]. These meetings are called to discuss matters of shared interest and to settle disputes between towns.


##### **PCs Reputation**

Completing quests for the Ten-Towns spreads the PCs reputation, for better or for worse.

- When the characters reach 3rd level, they begin to earn a healthy measure of respect from Ten-Towners who know of their exploits.
- When the characters reach 4th level, they are given a hero's welcome in the towns they've helped but are given the cold shoulder in towns they've ignored. They also learn about new adventure opportunities outside Ten-Towns (Adventures in [[Icewind Dale]])


##### **Sacrifices to Auril**

The desperate people of Ten-Towns, hoping to appease Auril so that summer can return to Icewind Dale, make sacrifices to the Frostmaiden on nights of the new moon. This is a new practice that started a little over a year ago, when it became clear that Auril was angry and summer would not be returning anytime soon. The town speakershave unanimously agreed to honor these practices, which they consider necessary evils, but would end them in a heartbeat if Auril were to be appeased or dealt with in some other way.

The nature of the sacrifices varies from town to town, but usually takes one of three forms:

- **Humanoid.** [[Bryn Shander]], [[Easthaven]], and [[Targos]] hold lotteries the afternoon before the new moon. The unlucky person whose name is drawn is sacrificed at nightfall. The ill-fated soul is stripped bare and either tied to a post or sent into the tundra to die. Accusations of rigged lotteries are common but usually not acted upon.
- **Food.** Smaller towns that can't afford to give up people give up their food instead. A day's catch of knucklehead trout is strung up on wooden racks a mile outside town, to be claimed by yeti and other creatures that embody Auril's wrath.
- **Warmth.** Towns that can't bring themselves to give up their people or their food forsake warmth for a night. No fires are lit between dusk and dawn, forcing locals to share body heat for warmth. Anyone who dares to light a fire is savagely beaten.
